offline-battle mod - World of Tanks 0.8.2
===================================================
Release 0.5 - battle results, medals and economy (2026-08-25)

Play World of Tanks 0.8.2 offline: no login, no server. You go straight to the
hangar, pick a tank and fight bots on the real maps.


What changed in this build
--------------------------
The post-battle screen. What the table reports, which medals you get, and what
a battle pays.

A. THE DAMAGE TABLE WAS REPORTING THE WRONG NUMBER
--------------------------------------------------
Every bot's damage column showed the damage that bot had RECEIVED, not what it
had dealt.

The offline build kept three loose counters, all stored on the tank that was
hit: how much the player had done to it, how many times, and how much everyone
else had done to it. That last one cannot answer "what did bot 12 deal" - there
is no shooter in it - and it was the number the results screen was handed.

Damage is now kept the way the client's own battle_results_shared.py wants it:
one record per (shooter, victim) pair, carrying the nine fields it names.
Every column of the team table is a sum over those pairs, so dealt and received
are two directions of the same record and cannot be swapped again.

That fixed a run of things that all had the same root:

  * Frags went to whoever had dealt the most damage to a tank, not to whoever
    fired the killing blow. A shot that finishes off a tank you had barely
    touched is your kill, and it was not being counted as one.
  * Shots were never counted at all. The counter existed, was reset at the
    start of every battle and read at the end, but nothing ever incremented it -
    so the shots column was always zero and every accuracy figure with it.
  * A ricochet or a bare track hit did not count as a hit.
  * he_hits, damageAssisted and fire were always zero although the mod has HE
    splash and a fire system.
  * crits was set to 1 when you killed the tank and 0 otherwise, which is not
    what the field means.
  * Bots' shots, hits and pierced were invented from the damage figure - the
    code said so: "Simulate some random shots and hits".

B. MEDALS
---------
There were none: the results always carried an empty list.

The thresholds ship with the client - arena_achievements.py has
ACHIEVEMENT_CONDITIONS in full - but the routine that applies them sits behind
`if IS_BASEAPP` and ran on the server, so it is rebuilt here against WG's own
numbers.

Both places the client draws medals are fed. The big panel on the personal tab
reads dossierPopUps, which is what it has always read - it never looks at the
'achievements' field - and the small icons on a team-table row read
'achievements'. Same medals, two shapes.

Kill-count bands behave: three kills is Pascucci, four is Dumitru, five or more
is Burda, and only the band you are in is awarded.

Two are deliberately never given. Supporter and Eagle Eye both want six
assists, and the mod cannot yet say which ally finished off something you had
spotted. An empty medal case is better than one filled from a number that means
something else.

C. CREDITS, XP AND SERVICE COSTS
--------------------------------
A battle paid a flat 10000 credits and 1000 XP, win or lose, whatever you did.

The cost side is now real, read out of this client's own files: repairCost per
hit point per vehicle (3.54 at tier 2, rising to 11.17 at tier 10), shell
prices per round, and consumable prices. Ammunition is billed per shell type,
so a magazine of gold rounds is charged in gold; every kit you actually fired
is bought again.

The earning side never shipped - that calculation ran on the server and WG has
never published the coefficients. What is documented is the structure, and that
is what is modelled: damage weighted by the tier difference to the victim, plus
kills, plus damage an ally dealt to something you spotted, plus base capture
and defence points, plus a survival bonus, and +50% for the winning team.

The coefficients are calibrated so that meeting the real costs above reproduces
the era's economy - mid tiers profit comfortably even in a loss, a mediocre
tier-8 loss lands near break-even, and a mediocre tier-10 loss costs about 20k.
Every one of them can be re-tuned from config.json without recompiling.

D. BOTS DECIDE AT 10 Hz INSTEAD OF EVERY FRAME
----------------------------------------------
A bot's obstacle feelers are the most expensive thing in the battle tick -
about 25 collision rays each, roughly 750 per frame with 30 bots, the largest
single item in the performance profile.

Deciding "left or right around this rock" does not need to happen thirty times
a second. Bots are now split into three groups and only one group casts rays on
any given frame; the other two keep steering on the memory that code already
maintained. Driving and turning still run every frame for every bot, so nothing
stutters. Set bot_ai_phases to 1 for the old behaviour.

E. A DAMAGED TRACK NO LONGER SLOWS THE TURN
-------------------------------------------
Turning the hull is engine work. A damaged - not thrown - track used to halve
the traverse rate; it now costs nothing, and a damaged engine is what slows the
turn instead. A THROWN track still stops the turn outright, through a separate
path that is unchanged.

Worth knowing: 0.8.2 ships no module-to-stat table at all. The client only
checks whether the engine, a track or the gun is DESTROYED. Everything about
what a merely damaged module costs is reconstructed, in this build as in the
last one.


Release 0.4 + aiming, ballistics and UI fixes (2026-08-05)
----------------------------------------------------------
Gunnery. Aiming, the shell's flight, and everything that draws or reports it.

1. THE GUN WAS AIMED AT THE SKY
-------------------------------
The single fault behind "the aiming point is much higher than it should be",
"the projectile flies higher", and "shells are going up".

The client works out what you are pointing at with a raycast, and that raycast
uses BigWorld.player().spaceID. Offline the player is the ACCOUNT, whose space
is not the space the battle map is loaded into - so the ray hit nothing, ever.
The client's fallback for "hit nothing" is to aim at a point straight out at the
shell's maximum range, and that is what the gun was given: a sky point at 720 m,
every frame, whatever was actually under your reticle.

So the gun elevated for a 720 m shot at a target 250 m away, and the round sailed
over everything. The mod now casts that ray itself, in the battle space, through
the exact screen pixel the reticle is drawn at - and it also tests the tanks
along it, since offline tanks are not part of the engine's collision scene and a
reticle on an enemy would otherwise aim at the ground behind him.

2. SHELLS NOW FLY THEIR REAL TRAJECTORY
---------------------------------------
The barrel is elevated to drop the shell onto the target. A straight line along
that elevated barrel therefore lands high and long by about twice the drop - a
few centimetres on a fast gun, but 4.2 m at 400 m for a KV-2's howitzer.

Three separate things were using that straight line and are now walking the real
parabola, chord by chord, the way the game's own gun marker and projectile mover
do it:

  * the aiming circle, so it shows where the shell truly lands
  * the hit test, so the round hits what the circle was on
  * the TRACER, which was the last one and the most visible: it was drawn to a
    straight-line end point, and the projectile mover then solved a launch that
    reached that too-high point AND paid for gravity, so the round you watched
    climbed well above the round that dealt the damage

The aiming circle also stops on tanks now, so it no longer slides onto the
ground behind the target you are aiming at.

3. THE AIMING CIRCLE ITSELF
---------------------------
Ported from the client's own dispersion model, which the offline build had only
approximated.

  * Aim time was roughly 3.6x too long and the circle never actually closed:
    it eased towards its ideal size instead of decaying and being caught by it.
    Measured on a KV-2: full bloom to aimed took 11.05 s, against 3.07 s for the
    real formula.
  * Bloom faded IN gradually and depended on your framerate. It now lands in the
    frame it happens, as it should.
  * Every shell was thrown with the AFTER-shot cone rather than the circle you
    aimed with - 4.85x too much scatter on every shot, however long you waited.
  * A single flick of the mouse pinned the turret-rotation term at maximum,
    because it measured the angle still to travel over one frame instead of the
    rotation actually performed.
  * Firing anything other than your first shell type used that first shell's
    muzzle velocity and gravity for aiming.

4. DAMAGE ARRIVES WITH THE SHELL
--------------------------------
Hit points, crits and kills were applied the instant you pulled the trigger,
about a second before a slow round reached its target. They now wait out the
shell's flight, for your rounds and for the bots'. Tracers also stop on the tank
they hit instead of flying on past it, and a round that arrives at a tank someone
else has already killed no longer steals the frag.

5. BOTS
-------
  * Bots aimed dead at their target while firing WITH gravity, so every bot
    round arced under its own aim point - and their straight hit test never
    noticed, so the shell you watched fall short was not the shell that hit you.
    They now aim and resolve on the real trajectory.
  * Bot accuracy was a single hardcoded number for every tank in the game. It
    now comes from the bot's own gun, scaled so the average is unchanged:
    accurate guns are sharper, derpy ones wilder.
  * A tank left at zero hit points could keep driving with its tracks turning.
    Any route to zero now ends in a proper death.

6. INTERFACE
------------
  * A used repair kit, med kit or extinguisher turned GREEN instead of going
    grey - the panel reads -1 as "this equipment is active".
  * Clicking a different shell type reloaded the gun immediately. As in the real
    game, the first click only selects the type for the next load; a second click
    on the same slot forces the swap.
  * An autoloader drew a full magazine while the gun was still reloading, after
    firing the last round of a shell type that ran out.
  * Modules destroyed in the PREVIOUS battle stayed red on a fresh tank.
  * Broken-module icons vanished when the spectator switched from an ally back
    to yourself.
  * Damage dealt to a tank that was not currently spotted left no trace at all -
    no number, and its health bar came back showing the old value.

7. VISUALS
----------
  * Allied tanks had no green silhouette. The game keeps one outline palette and
    sets it up from a handler that only runs on a real player Avatar, so offline
    the "friendly" colour was never registered.
  * Enemy silhouettes showed THROUGH allied tanks, because the occlusion check
    only tested terrain and buildings.
  * Destroyed tanks did not burn: the wreck flames are authored at a hardpoint
    that the world-space effect model does not have.
  * Leaving a battle in sniper mode could leave the garage zoomed in.

Everything else is Release 0.4, unchanged.


Install
-------
This zip starts at  scripts/  and  gui/ .
Extract it INTO:  <WoT 0.8.2 game root>/res_mods/0.8.2/
so the files land at:  res_mods/0.8.2/scripts/client/gui/mods/mod_offhangar.py

>>> UPDATING FROM AN EARLIER BUILD? DELETE TWO FILES FIRST <<<

    res_mods/0.8.2/scripts/client/gui/mods/offhangar/offline_battle.pyc
    res_mods/0.8.2/scripts/client/gui/mods/mod_offhangar.pyc

This zip deliberately ships those two modules as source WITHOUT their compiled
.pyc, because the fixes are source changes and the compiled form has to be
regenerated with Python 2.6. The game prefers a .pyc over a .py, and extracting
an archive does not remove files - so an old .pyc left in place will shadow the
fix and nothing will change. Delete both, then extract. Every other module still
ships its .pyc and loads normally.

Start the game with WorldOfTanks.exe. If the mod loaded you go straight to the
offline hangar instead of the login screen.

To uninstall, delete  res_mods/0.8.2/scripts  and  res_mods/0.8.2/gui .
Your settings survive in  <game root>/offhangar_user/  - delete that folder too
if you want a clean slate.


Settings
--------
Your editable copy is created on first launch at

    <game root>/offhangar_user/config.json

It is OUTSIDE res_mods, so updating or deleting the mod never touches it.
Options that a mod update adds later fall back to their defaults until you add
them to your file; the shipped defaults live in config_defaults.json next to
the mod and are documented there.

The ones people change most:

  nickname                your in-game name
  bots_per_team           15  (15 vs 15)
  spotting_enabled        enemies must be spotted to be seen
  perfect_accuracy        shells always land in the centre of the circle
  prebattle_countdown_seconds / auto_spawn_delay_seconds

New in this build:

  bot_ai_phases           3 - how many frames a bot waits between steering
                          decisions. 3 means it re-decides about ten times a
                          second instead of thirty, which is where most of the
                          bot cost went. Driving stays per-frame. 1 restores the
                          old behaviour; above 3 they start clipping corners.

  economy_tuning          {} - overrides any earning coefficient without
                          recompiling (restart only). The costs are real client
                          data and are not tunable. Keys are documented in
                          config_defaults.json next to the mod.

Carried over from the previous build:

  ballistic_shells        true - shells follow their real gravity-bent path for
                          both the aiming circle and the hit test. Set false to
                          go back to a straight line along the barrel.
  max_engine_sound_loops  10 - how many tanks may hold looping engine and track
                          sound at once (the client mixes 64 sounds in total).
  vehicle_sound_volume    0.6 - weights engine and track noise down for the
                          battle only; your saved settings are untouched.


In battle
---------
  O / P / L   spawn a bot where you aim - your own tank as an enemy clone /
              a random enemy / a random ally
  K           leave the battle


Module and crew damage
----------------------
Shells damage modules and crew the way the era did: every device has its own HP
pool from the vehicle descriptor, its own hit chance from the game's material
table, and repairs itself back to roughly half over time. Damaged modules cost
performance, destroyed ones stop working. Fires start from the engine or a
holed fuel tank and burn out on their own. Repair kits, med kits and the fire
extinguisher work, and the damage panel and crew voice lines follow.

Interior modules and crew have no collision geometry in the 0.8.2 client, so
their hit boxes come from a per-vehicle profile set covering 251 vehicles.
Switch it off with  internal_layout_profiles: false  to fall back to a coarser
compartment model.

  module_test_mode: true    bot shells roll every module and crew crit but take
                            no hull HP off you, so you can watch the system work
                            without dying. Turn it off for normal play.


Notes
-----
Debug logging is OFF by default. Turn on  debug_logging  in your config.json if
you want the mod to write diagnostics into python.log. With it on, each battle
ends with three lines worth reading:

    Ledger: pairs=... kills=... spotted=...
    Medals earned: ...
    economy: xp=... credits=... repair=... ammo=(...)

What is real and what is reconstructed, so nobody has to guess:

  REAL, read from this client's own files - per-vehicle repair cost, shell and
  consumable prices, the per-vehicle XP and credit multipliers, the factor
  chain the results screen applies on top, and the medal thresholds.

  RECONSTRUCTED, because it ran on the server and was never shipped - how much
  a point of damage earns, and what a damaged (as opposed to destroyed) module
  costs you. Both are calibrated rather than invented, and both are tunable
  from config.json.

0.8.2 is a 32-bit client. Long non-stop sessions across many different maps
slowly grow memory - if it gets sluggish after a lot of battles, restart the
client.

The X-ray overlay (internal_xray_overlay) draws module and crew boxes through
the armour. It is a debug view for offline battles and is OFF by default; the
module is not even loaded while it is off. Do not turn it on in a client you
log into a live server with.


======================================================================
BOTS NOW NAVIGATE (new in this build)
-------------------------------------
Bots used to drive straight at the nearest enemy. That was the only notion of
"where to go" in the mod, so both teams converged on the middle of the map and
died there. They now pathfind, and they go to places that suit the tank they
are driving.

This applies to maps that have been measured. On those, routes are laid out
for all five classes on both teams: artillery holds well back, tank destroyers
set up about a third of the way over, mediums and heavies push past the middle,
lights go furthest forward. Several routes per class, down separate lanes, so
fifteen heavies do not drive in single file.

ON EVERY OTHER MAP NOTHING CHANGES AND NOTHING IS MEASURED. Bots behave exactly
as they did before. Nothing map-specific ships with the mod, so out of the box
no battle is slower than it used to be - you opt a map in when you want it.

Where things are kept
    <game>\offhangar_user
av_dump\<map>.grid          your own, if you make one
    <game>\offhangar_user
av_dump\<map>.routes.json   the generated routes

Yours always wins over the shipped one, and both live outside res_mods next to
config.json, so mod updates leave them alone.

There is nothing to configure. Painting a map in the editor is what asks for it
to be measured; a map you have not painted is never touched. ("auto_routes":
false turns the generated routes off and uses only what you painted.)


EDITING WHERE BOTS GO (optional)
--------------------------------
If you want to decide the routes yourself, the editor ships with the mod at
res_mods .8.2\scripts\offhangar_tools\.

    python res_mods .8.2\scripts\offhangar_tools\paint_map.py

Needs Python 3 with Pillow and Tk (pip install pillow). It finds your game
folder by itself when run from inside it.

You can draw
    Points  a destination for a team and class
    Routes  an ordered path - "go THIS way", the part a pathfinder cannot guess
    Avoid   a keep-out area, which bots will route around

A map must have been PLAYED once before you can edit it properly - that is when
its mesh is measured. Open a map with no mesh and the editor says so, and offers
which map you play stays your choice, and the mesh is made whenever you next
happen to play that one. After that battle, reopen the map here and it is fully
editable.

Use Import to load the routes the game generated, so you start from what the
bots already do instead of a blank map. Anything you paint and save overrides
the generated routes for that map.

Your painted profiles live in
    res_mods .8.2\scripts\client\gui\mods\offhangar\painted
Nothing here is required. Ignore the editor entirely and the bots still
navigate - you will just get the automatic routes rather than your own.
